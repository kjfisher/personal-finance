Two ways interest works · Notes box 2

Simple vs. compound

Simple interest

Interest is paid only on the money you started with (the principal). Same amount every year.

Compound interest

Interest is paid on the principal AND on the interest you already earned. Growth earns growth.

Same rate, different rule

Compound interest isn't a higher rate.

  • Both accounts below earn 10%.
  • Simple: 10% of the original $10,000, every year.
  • Compound: 10% of whatever the balance is now, including last year's interest.

The formulas · Notes box 4

Skip the table: use a formula

Simple: I = P × r × t

I = interest · P = principal (starting amount) · r = yearly rate as a decimal · t = years. Then add I to P for the total.

Compound: A = P(1 + r)^t

A = final amount · P = principal · r = yearly rate as a decimal · t = years. The ^t means "multiply by (1 + r), t times."

Check it against the chart

The flip side · Notes box 6

Compounding can work against you

Debt

A credit card at 24%: 72 ÷ 24 = about 3 years for what you owe to double if you don't pay it down.

Inflation

Prices rising 3% a year: 72 ÷ 3 = about 24 years for prices to double. Cash under the mattress buys half as much.
