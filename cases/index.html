Personal Finance · Units 1–10

Case Studies

Every unit ends with a case study: a real-life money decision you judge and back up with a number. It's its own grade, separate from the quiz.

The 4-box rubric

Each box is worth 0, 0.5 or 1 point, for a total of 4.

ClaimSmart, not smart, or PARTLY smart?Name what's smart AND what isn't.
CalculateOne number that proves it.Show your work, and label it (per month? per year?).
EvidenceWhat the number means.Plus one more fact from the unit.
AdviceA specific next step.With a number and 2 word-bank words.

Weak advice: “Spend less and save more.” Strong advice: “Cut eating out from $410 to $200 and set up autopay of $210 to the HYSA: that's $2,520 a year.”

Unit 2 uses Claim · Evidence · Reasoning (with 2 word-bank words in the reasoning).

Unit 10 · Final Case Study

Your team advises a real-looking client: find monthly take-home pay and the shortfall, fix things in the advising order, handle a Life Event, then pitch your plan.

  • Days 40–42: Team Plan & Pitch (16 points) + your own Advisor Memo (16 points, your own grade).
  • Days 43–44: Classroom Poster (16 points).
  • The advising order: stop the bleeding → a starter cushion → free money (the match) → bigger goals.
