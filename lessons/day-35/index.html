5min · 0–5

Bell ringer

$1,000,000 today or a penny that doubles every day for 30 days?

  • Slides 1–4. Students write a pick and a reason in Notes box 1 before the reveal.
  • Slide 4 chart: the penny ends at $5,368,709.12; half of it arrives on day 30. Tie back to Unit 1.
5min · 5–10

Target + Rule of 72

72 ÷ rate = years to double.

  • Slides 5–6. Read the target and success criteria aloud. Reveal 4% → 18 years, 8% → 9, then credit card debt at 24% doubling in 3.
20min · 10–30

Mini-lesson: stocks & bonds

Stock = owner (riskier). Bond = lender (steadier).

  • Slides 7–12 with Notes boxes 2–3. Box 2 is in students' own words: don't let them copy the slide word for word.
  • Slide 9 chart (illustration): stocks ended higher but dropped hard in year 5.
  • Slide 10 turn and talk (2 min). Cold-call 2 pairs.
  • Slide 11: students rank the ladder in box 3 BEFORE you reveal.
  • Misconception: "Low risk means no risk." Bonds can lose value, and savings can lose to inflation.
15min · 30–45

Read a stock page + ticker hunt

Look up 3 companies you know on Yahoo Finance.

  • Slide 13: walk through the Brightside Snacks (BRSN) stock page terms. It's a made-up company.
  • Slide 14: students fill Notes box 4. Companies with no ticker are private.
10min · 45–55

Worked example + you try

Gain or loss on 20 shares of BRSN, then 12 shares of PKOD.

  • Slides 15–16, Notes box 5. Model BRSN (+$180, +60%), then students try PKOD before you reveal: −$72, −15%. Point out that stocks can lose money.
15min · 55–70

Diversification

Don't put all your eggs in one basket.

  • Slide 17 chart (illustration): one stock $1,000 → $351; 20 stocks $1,000 → $1,653.
  • Slide 18 turn and talk, then slide 19: students write the cousin advice in box 6 (2–3 sentences using risk and diversification).
  • Collect 3 strong answers to read aloud tomorrow.
10min · 70–80

Summary + Invest Corners

Finish the summary frame, then play Invest Corners.

  • Slide 20: box 7 summary frame; two share-outs.
  • Slide 21: Invest Corners to review stock vs. bond and risk.
5min · 80–85

Exit ticket

Which is riskier, a stock or a bond? Why?

  • Slide 22; students answer in the exit ticket box. Look for: a stock's value can rise or fall a lot because you own part of a company; a bond is a loan that pays interest.
  • Sort quickly: got it / developing / reteach.

Differentiation

  • Support: pre-filled Yahoo Finance table for 3 companies; sentence frame "A stock is ___, a bond is ___."
  • Extension: compare the 52-week range of two stocks and explain which is more volatile.
  • EL: picture cards for stock (slice of a pizza = slice of a company) and bond (IOU).
