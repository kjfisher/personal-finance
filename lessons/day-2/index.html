5min · 0–5

Bell ringer: penny reveal

$1,000,000 today or 1¢ that doubles every day for 30 days?

  • Notes box 1: students write a pick and a reason, then reveal the chart. The penny ends at $5,368,709.12; day 20 is only $5,242.88.
  • Ask: why does it look like nothing is happening for 20 days?
5min · 5–10

Target

Simple vs. compound, and the Rule of 72.

  • Read the target and success criteria aloud.
10min · 10–20

Mini-lesson: simple vs. compound

Simple = interest on the principal only. Compound = interest on interest too.

  • Notes box 2 (two-column). Keep it concrete: same $10,000, same 10%, different rules.
  • Misconception: "Compound interest is a higher rate." Same rate; the difference is what the rate is applied to.
10min · 20–30

Account A vs. Account B

$10,000 at 10%: simple (A) vs. compound (B), year by year.

  • Notes box 3: students fill years 1–3 before each reveal. Year 2: A earns $1,000, B earns $1,100 ($100 more, interest on last year's interest).
  • Chart: after 10 years A = $20,000, B = $25,937.
15min · 30–45

The formulas

Simple: I = P × r × t. Compound: A = P(1 + r)^t.

  • Notes box 4. Show both formulas give the chart's answers: A: I = $10,000 × 0.10 × 10 = $10,000 → $20,000. B: $10,000 × 1.10^10 = $25,937.42.
  • You try (box 4): a $2,500 CD at 4% for 3 years. Simple: I = $300 → $2,800. Compound: $2,500 × 1.04^3 = $2,812.16. Show the ^ (or x^y) key on the calculator.
  • Then the 6% table: $1,000 for 10/20/30/40 years. Simple $1,600 / $2,200 / $2,800 / $3,400; compound $1,791 / $3,207 / $5,743 / $10,286. Ask: when does the gap explode?
  • The year-by-year table and the formula are the same math; both earn full credit on quizzes.
10min · 45–55

Rule of 72

72 ÷ rate ≈ years to double: the no-calculator shortcut.

  • Notes box 5: worked example (4% → 18 years), then students try 6%, 12% and 10% before each reveal (12, 6, 7.2).
  • Doubling math: $5,000 at 12% doubles every 6 years → $40,000 in 18 years (3 doubles).
10min · 55–65

The flip side: debt + inflation

Compounding can work against you.

  • Credit card at 24%: debt doubles in about 3 years. Inflation at 3%: prices double in about 24 years, so cash under the mattress loses buying power.
  • Notes box 6: one sentence on how compounding can hurt.
15min · 65–80

Practice games

Growth on Growth (solo), then Compound Corners.

  • Growth on Growth makes new numbers every round. Then project Compound Corners for movement.
5min · 80–85

Exit ticket

Compound growth means you earn money on…

  • Look for: your principal AND the interest/growth you've already earned.
