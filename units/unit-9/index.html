Key vocabulary

Investment vehicleA product used to earn returns: CDs and bonds (lower risk) up to stocks (higher risk).
Risk & rewardThe greater the risk, the greater the chance of reward, or loss.
Diversified portfolioA mix of many investment types so no single one can sink you.
StockOwnership of a fraction of a company. Units are called shares.
BondA loan you make to a government or company that pays you interest.
Ticker symbolA short code for a company's stock, like COST for Costco.
IndexA collection of investments that tracks a market, like the S&P 500, NASDAQ or Dow (30 companies).
Index fundTracks an index. Passively managed, so fees are low.
Mutual fundA pool of money run by professional managers. Actively managed, usually higher fees.
ETFExchange-traded fund: trades like a stock all day. Most track an index, with very low fees and no minimum.
Expense ratioA fund's yearly fee, as a percent of what you have invested.
Target-date fundAutomatically gets more conservative as your retirement year gets closer.
401(k)Employer retirement plan. Traditional = pre-tax; Roth 401(k) = after-tax. Employers may match.
IRA / Roth IRARetirement accounts you open yourself. Roth: taxed now, qualified withdrawals tax-free.
Social SecurityMost workers pay 6.2% of wages; collect as early as 62, more if you wait.
Rule of 7272 ÷ rate ≈ years for money to double.
BeneficiaryThe person you name to receive an account (401(k), IRA, life insurance) when you die.
WillA legal document that says who gets your property and who cares for minor children.
ProbateThe court process that checks a will, pays debts and hands out property.
TrustA legal arrangement where a trustee manages property for beneficiaries; a living trust avoids probate.
