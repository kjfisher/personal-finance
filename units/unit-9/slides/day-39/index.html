Common mistakes · Notes box 2

Investing traps

  • Rule of 72 gives years to DOUBLE. $4,000 → $16,000 is TWO doubles.
  • Match total = your % + the employer's % (only up to the match limit).
  • Fee difference = balance × fee A − balance × fee B (use decimals: 0.8% = 0.008).
  • A beneficiary form beats the will for that account.

Practice case · Notes box 3

Kai D., 25, puts 6% in his 401(k) (his employer matches up to 4%). When the market dropped 25%, he moved his whole $18,000 into the plan's cash fund (0%): "I'll go back in when it's safe." The stock index fund has averaged about 8%.

Smart, not smart, or partly smart? Use the Rule of 72: what could $18,000 become in the index fund, and when?
Grade these two answers · Notes box 3

Score each box 0, 0.5 or 1

Sample A

"Smart. The market is risky and he stopped losing money. Cash is safe, so his retirement is protected."

Sample B

"Partly smart. Putting in 6% gets the full 4% match, which is smart. But moving to cash after a 25% drop locks in the loss. At 8%, money doubles in 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years, so his $18,000 could be about $36,000 in 9 years; in cash at 0% it stays $18,000. He's 25 with 40 years to go, so he should move back to the low-fee index or target-date fund and keep investing every paycheck."
