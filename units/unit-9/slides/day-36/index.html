What's an index?

An index is a scoreboard for a group of stocks.

  • S&P 500: 500 large U.S. companies
  • Dow Jones Industrial Average: 30 well-known companies
  • NASDAQ Composite: thousands of companies, heavy on technology
Funds

A fund is a basket. Many people pool money to buy many investments at once.

  • One purchase = a small piece of hundreds of companies.
  • Every fund charges a yearly fee called an expense ratio.
Notes box 3

Who picks the investments?

Index fund = autopilot

Copies an index like the S&P 500 · passively managed · very low fees (often under 0.2%)

Mutual fund = pilot

Professional managers pick (actively managed) · higher fees (often 0.5%–1%+) · most don't beat their index over time

ETF · Notes box 3

An ETF (exchange-traded fund) is a fund you buy and sell like a stock.

  • It trades all day while the market is open, at a price that changes like a stock's.
  • Most ETFs track an index. An S&P 500 ETF is an index fund in ETF form.
  • Usually very low fees, and no minimum: buy one share, or a fraction of a share in many apps.
  • Index = the strategy (copy the scoreboard). ETF = the package it comes in.
Target-date fund · Notes box 3

Pick the year you'll retire. It shifts for you.

Typical glide path; each fund company sets its own

Young investors have time to ride out drops, so the fund holds mostly stocks. It moves toward bonds as the target year gets closer.
