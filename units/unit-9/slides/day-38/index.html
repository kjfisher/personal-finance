Why it matters

If you don't make a plan, state law makes one for you.

  • Your estate = everything you own minus what you owe.
  • Dying without a will is called dying intestate. State law then decides who inherits.
  • Even an 18-year-old has an estate: a bank account, a car, a 401(k), digital accounts.
Beneficiaries · Notes box 2

A beneficiary is the person you name to get an account when you die.

  • Your 401(k), IRA, life insurance and many bank accounts ask you to name one, often on your first day of work.
  • These accounts go straight to the named person, outside your will.
  • Update them after big life events: marriage, divorce, a new child.

Wills · Notes box 4

What a will does

Will

A legal document that says who gets your property, who becomes guardian of your minor children, and who carries out your wishes (the executor).

Probate

The court process that checks the will, pays your debts and hands out what's left. It can take months, and its records are public.

Making a valid will

In Illinois, a valid will needs:

  • You: at least 18 and of sound mind
  • It in writing, signed by you
  • Two witnesses who sign it too
  • A review every few years and after big life events
Trusts · Notes box 4

Will vs. living trust

Will

Goes through probate · public record · takes effect at death · cheaper to set up

Living trust

Avoids probate · private · can manage money for years, even after you die · costs more to set up

Trust roles · Notes box 4

Every trust has three roles.

  • Grantor: the person who creates the trust and puts property in it
  • Trustee: the person who manages the property by the trust's rules
  • Beneficiary: the person who receives the property or money
