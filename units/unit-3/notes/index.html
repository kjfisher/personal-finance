Common mistakes (check yourself)

  • Weekly × 4 is NOT monthly: use × 52 ÷ 12
  • Expenses > take-home = a shortfall, not savings ability
  • Zero-based = $0 left WITHOUT a job, not $0 saved
  • 50/30/20 uses take-home pay, not gross

Day 13 · Unit 3 Review

Learning target: I can show what I know about budgeting.

1 · Bell ringer

A friend says: "I budget groceries at $70 × 4 = $280 a month." What's wrong, and what's the real number?

2 · Make the Call (practice)

Nia R. takes home $2,700. On payday $270 goes to savings automatically. Her plan: rent $1,150, car $340, insurance $135, phone $95, groceries $70 a week (she wrote $280), fun $370, subscriptions $60. She says, "Every dollar has a job: $0 left!"

Claim: smart, not smart, or partly smart? What's smart AND what isn't?
Calculate: her real monthly total (savings included)
Evidence + Advice (use 2 course words)
Day 13 · page 2

Grade two answers (0, 0.5 or 1 per box)

Sample A: "Not smart. Nia spends too much on fun and her phone. She should make a budget and save more money."

Sample B: "Partly smart. Paying herself first and giving every dollar a job is smart, but she used $70 × 4. Real groceries are $70 × 52 ÷ 12 = $303.33, so her plan totals $2,723.33: $23.33 over every month ($280 a year) with no cushion. She should cut $25 of fun or switch to a cheaper phone plan and keep her $270 savings."
